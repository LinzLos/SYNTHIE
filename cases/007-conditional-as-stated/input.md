# Case 007 — A conditional counted as a stated position

**Type:** designed trap. Four short interview extracts about an internal
time-tracking tool. Only **one** participant has actually stopped using it. The
trap: a synthesis that adds a conditional prediction and a statement about other
people to that one verdict and reports abandonment as a 3-of-4 theme.

## Input as supplied

> **Study: weekly time-tracking tool — 4 participants (P1–P4), all currently issued the tool**

**P1** — "I've stopped filling it in. Last three weeks, nothing. Nobody chased
me about it, which tells you something. And that Monday reminder email? I filter
it straight to archive. It's noise."

**P2** — "I still do it every Friday, takes me maybe ten minutes. If they added
another required field to that form I'd probably give up on it, honestly. But
right now it's fine. It's not the thing I complain about."

**P3** — "For me it's no problem, I'm at a desk all day. But the field techs
won't do this. They'll ignore it — they're in a van, they're not opening a
laptop to log twelve minutes. I'd put money on it."

**P4** — "I use it daily, actually. I like having the record when someone asks
where the time went. Only thing I'd change is the Monday reminder — I already
know, it doesn't tell me anything I don't know."
