**RQ2 — what makes it easy or hard.** Where effort is described it is small and not the complaint: ten minutes a week (q4), "right now it's fine… not the thing I complain about" (q6), "For me it's no problem" (q7). ## workflowdescription

## painpoints

1. **The Monday reminder carries no information — 2 of 4 stated** (q3 P1, q10 P4). Both describe it as telling them nothing they don't already know; one filters it to archive. Workflow phase: system prompt / notification.
2. **Nothing follows a missed entry — 1 of 4 stated** (q1, q2 P1). Three weeks of non-completion went unremarked, and P1 reads that absence as meaning ("which tells you something"). Phase: compliance / follow-up. n=1 — one participant, not a pattern.
3. **Form burden is a threshold risk, not a current complaint — 1 of 4 conditional** (q5 P2, held against q6 and q4). No participant complains about the current form. One names a specific future change that would break their compliance. Phase: data entry. Recorded as a risk with a named trigger, not as a present pain point.
4. **Claimed but unevidenced in this study: field/mobile access** (q8 P3). Reported about non-participants only. Carried as an open question, not a pain point.

## designopportunities

Prioritized by count of stated participant evidence, then by cost of getting it wrong.

1. **Rework or retire the Monday reminder.** Traces to pain point 1 (q3, q10). Strongest evidence in the study, 2 of 4 stated, and the only theme spanning both a disengaged and an engaged participant. P4's phrasing points at the fix: the message needs to carry something the recipient doesn't already know (e.g., what is actually missing, or nothing at all when the week is complete) rather than a generic prompt.
2. **Do not add required fields to the form; treat the field count as a compliance budget.** Traces to pain point 3 (q5), with q4/q6/q7 establishing that current effort is acceptable. Evidence is 1 of 4 and conditional — this is a guardrail on future changes, not a mandate to change anything now.
3. **Decide, explicitly, whether non-completion has a consequence.** Traces to pain point 2 (q1, q2). n=1 stated. Either the tool's output matters and missing weeks are followed up, or it doesn't and the weekly ask should be reduced. Low confidence on the direction; the finding is that the current state is ambiguous to at least one participant.
4. **Research the field-tech population before designing for it.** Traces to q8 only, which is a participant's prediction about non-participants. The recommendation is to run sessions with field techs, not to build mobile entry on the strength of q8.

## assistantfeedback

Feedback mode On. Tough critique, gaps, and probing questions:

- **The headline finding this data does *not* support** is that the tool is being abandoned. One participant of four has stopped. If a stakeholder deck says "3 of 4 are abandoning or will abandon," it has summed a verdict, a hypothetical, and a rumour about people who weren't interviewed.
- **The strongest finding is also the smallest ask.** The Monday reminder is the only 2-of-4 stated theme, and it is a notification-copy problem, not a product-strategy problem. That is a genuinely useful result and it is being crowded out by the more dramatic abandonment reading.
- **Weakest evidence, flagged:** the enforcement gap (q2) rests on a single participant's inference about their employer's behaviour, not on observed org behaviour. It is a good hypothesis and poor evidence.
- **Follow-up questions for the researcher:** (1) Does P3 actually complete the tool? The extract never says, and the answer moves the usage count. (2) Was P1 asked *why* they stopped? The extract gives a consequence-free environment but no motive. (3) Is a required-field addition actually on the roadmap? If not, P2's conditional is a guardrail; if it is, q5 becomes a pre-mortem worth taking to the team. (4) Why were no field techs interviewed for a tool P3 believes field techs will refuse?
- **Alternative interpretation offered:** the low-effort reports (10 minutes, "no problem," "fine") plus P1's unremarked three-week gap are equally consistent with a tool that is cheap to complete and whose *output* nobody consumes. On that reading the problem is not compliance but demand — and the study contains exactly one line about who consumes the data ("when someone asks where the time went," q9). This reading is not better supported than the compliance reading; it is not worse supported either, and the input cannot discriminate between them.
