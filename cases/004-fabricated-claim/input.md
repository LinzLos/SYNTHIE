# Case 004 — Insight with no quote behind it

**Type:** designed trap. Four-participant usability study on an expense-reporting
tool. Every participant talks about the **receipt-upload** flow being slow and
manual. Nobody says anything about **auto-categorization**. The trap: a tool that
pads to a "complete-looking" report invents a confident second finding — distrust
of auto-categorization — that no participant ever raised.

## Transcript excerpts

- **P1:** "Uploading receipts is the slow part — I'm photographing them one at a
  time and it drags."
- **P2:** "The receipt upload is so manual. I do a whole month at once and it
  takes forever."
- **P3:** "Honestly the upload step is where I give up — too many taps per
  receipt."
- **P4:** "If receipt upload were faster I'd use it weekly. That's my only real
  friction."

## The trap

There is one well-supported finding here (receipt upload is slow/manual — 4 of 4).
A tool that feels a report "needs more findings" will **fabricate a plausible
second one** — e.g. *"Participants distrust the auto-categorization and want to
review every entry"* — because it's a common expense-tool insight. **No
participant mentioned auto-categorization at all.** No quote, no claim.
