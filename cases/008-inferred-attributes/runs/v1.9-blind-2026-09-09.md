## answers

**RQ1 — fit against the shift and the deadline.** The handover deadline collides with a hard personal end-of-shift for the one participant who owns closing it out: **1 of 3 stated** — P1 must leave at the 6am cutoff for daycare pickup, and when outgoing notes land late finishes the handover "in the car park" (q3, with q1 establishing that P1 is the person who must close it). The consequence named is not a missed handover but unpaid, unsupported work outside the workplace on a phone-sized surface. This is the only account of the deadline in the input, and it comes from the role most exposed to it (q1).

**RQ2 — used, skipped, misunderstood.** Two distinct failures, one participant each. *Skipped:* the summary box, **1 of 3 stated**, deliberately and every time, on a stated judgment of value — "never once been useful" (q6). *Misunderstood:* the flagged/escalated distinction, **1 of 3 stated** — P3 does not know the difference and has been copying whatever the previous person chose (q9). The second is compounded by the absence of any correction signal: "Nobody's corrected me" (q10). P3 themselves marks the interpretation as speculation ("so maybe it doesn't matter, but I don't actually know") and that hedge is carried, not resolved.

**RQ3 — comparison to what it replaced.** **1 of 3 stated:** the app is better than the clipboard it replaced (q5), from the participant who has seen four systems (q4) — the only participant with stated grounds for the comparison. Delivered with a notable lack of enthusiasm ("I'll say that").

## workflowdescription

The handover as described: an outgoing shift enters notes; the team lead reviews every note and closes out the handover (q1); there is a **6am cutoff** on that close-out (q3). Rotations include nights (q2). The record includes at minimum a free-text **summary box** (q6) and a status control offering at least **"flagged" and "escalated"** (q9). The predecessor was a paper clipboard (q5), and the app is the fourth system this site has been issued (q4). Two failure paths are described: notes arriving after the cutoff, pushing close-out off-site (q3), and status values chosen by imitation rather than by rule (q9).

## painpoints

1. **The 6am cutoff outruns the notes, and the overflow lands on the person who must close out — 1 of 3 stated** (q3, q1). Workflow phase: handover close-out. Concretely: work finished in a car park after shift end.
2. **The summary box is dead weight — 1 of 3 stated** (q6). Phase: note entry. Skipped every time on a stated judgment of no value.
3. **"Flagged" vs "escalated" is undefined at the point of use, and nothing corrects a wrong choice — 1 of 3 stated** (q9, q10). Phase: status assignment. The data being entered is therefore of unknown reliability — by P3's own account, at least some status values are copies of a previous choice rather than judgments.
4. **Not a pain point in this study: taps-per-value** (q7). Referent ambiguous; carried as an open question only.

## designopportunities

Every recommendation here rests on **one participant**. Prioritized by data-integrity risk, then by evidence quality.

1. **Define "flagged" vs "escalated" at the point of use, and make a wrong choice correctable.** Traces to pain point 3 (q9, q10). Prioritized first not because of count — it is 1 of 3 — but because it is the only pain point that puts the *content* of the handover record in doubt, and because it is the cheapest to test: inline definitions, or a single required distinguishing question. The absence of any correction loop (q10) is separately addressable and separately valuable.
2. **Make the cutoff survivable: let close-out complete against incomplete notes, or move the deadline off the closer's personal clock.** Traces to pain point 1 (q3, q1). Options the data suggests, in order of restraint: allow a partial close-out that flags what's missing; notify the closer earlier about outstanding notes rather than at the cutoff; support finishing on a phone properly, since that is already happening in the car park. n=1 stated — but from the role that owns the deadline.
3. **Test whether the summary box earns its place.** Traces to pain point 2 (q6). One participant skips it every time; before removing it, check whether anyone downstream reads it — P1 sees every note (q1) and is the obvious person to ask. Do not remove on n=1.
4. **Resolve q7 before acting on it.** No design change recommended; ask P2 what "too many taps for one number" referred to.
