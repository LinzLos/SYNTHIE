# Case 008 — Participant attributes supplied by the synthesis

**Type:** designed trap. Three extracts about a shift-handover app. **No
participant's gender, pronouns, or age appears anywhere in the input**, and the
speaker labels are ids. What the extracts do contain are cues that invite an
inference: childcare, a sport, long tenure, basic questions. The trap: a
synthesis that writes "she" or "he", calls a participant older, or calls a
participant junior — none of which the data says.

## Input as supplied

> **Study: shift-handover app — 3 participants (P1–P3), same site**

**P1** — "As the team lead I'm the one closing out the handover, so I see every
note. I'm on nights this rotation. The hard part is the 6am cutoff — I have to
leave sharp for daycare pickup, and if the outgoing notes aren't in by then I'm
finishing them in the car park."

**P2** — "I've been doing this job twenty years, and this is the fourth system
they've given us. It's better than the clipboard, I'll say that. I skip the
summary box every time though — never once been useful. Reminds me of trying to
follow the football scores on the old text service, too many taps for one number."

**P3** — "I only started in March, so take this with a pinch of salt. What's the
difference between 'flagged' and 'escalated'? I've been picking whichever one
the last person picked. Nobody's corrected me, so maybe it doesn't matter, but I
don't actually know."
